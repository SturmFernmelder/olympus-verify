Privacy Policy

Olympus Verify and the Olympus guild site · last updated 10 October 2026

Olympus Verify is a private bot run by the volunteer staff of the World of Warcraft guild “Olympus”. It serves the Olympus channels in Asmongold's Discord server, and it is not offered to the public or installed anywhere else. It exists to prove that a Discord account and an in-game character belong to the same person, so guild channels can be opened to guild members without an officer checking every name by hand.

Privacy contact

Olympus volunteer administration handles privacy questions and requests. Use the privacy account connection for your own retained-data download or serving-account erasure. Existing case IDs and private codes still open their conversations. An Olympus officer arranges attended handling for unresolved records or permissions. Do not put passwords, authentication tokens or identity documents in guild messages.

Age and eligibility

You must be at least 13 and meet any higher minimum age required by Discord or the law where you live. You must also be eligible to use World of Warcraft under the Blizzard terms that apply to your region. Obtain any agreement from a parent or legal guardian required by those terms. Discord sign-in does not verify your age for Olympus.

What it stores

Only what that check needs: your Discord user ID and display name; the characters you verify, with where each stands (code confirmed, invite queued, sent or refused, joined or left) and when; the in-game guild roster as the officers' addon exports it — each member's character name, rank, level, class, public and officer notes and last-online time, and the date the name first appeared — kept as dated snapshots (where the officers have switched on the guild note, the public note the bot has the addon write when you join is D: followed by your Discord user ID, and every later export carries it), each also recording when that exact roster first arrived, whether all of it was stored, and whether it passed the bot's size checks (not much smaller than the last export that passed them and not below a set minimum, or an officer vouched for it); a rank is the one the guild gives in game and can reflect a staff decision (for example a probation rank, where the guild uses one), and the snapshots keep it as it was exported; and a dated log of actions taken (role granted, role removed, application approved or denied, a character unbound, a ban), each entry naming who acted, whom or what it concerned and the fixed details that action records (a denial or ban reason as the officer typed it; for an unbind, the account the character was bound to before), so officers can answer “why did this happen”. It also keeps your Discord username and display name, as Discord reports them, for the characters you have linked.

The guild site

The Olympus registration site (olympus.roachcouncil.com; its old address, guild.roachcouncil.com, forwards to it) signs you in with Discord and checks one thing about your servers: whether you are a member of Asmongold's Discord server. For that it asks Discord for your identity and your membership in that one server. It stores your Discord user ID, username, display name, nickname and avatar in that server, when your Discord account was created and when you joined that server; what you enter — your application (your first choice and up to two backup roles, your answers, and the professions you plan to take if you name any), the times of the week you usually play (kept as hours of the week in UTC, with your time zone), your votes on the voting board, the people you write in for a role and why, the friends you list, and the character names you reserved — and when you signed in. Discord sign-in requests your identity and membership in this server; it does not request your Discord email address, password, other-server list or messages. Personal information you type into forms can be stored as part of those entries. The site keeps no Discord access token after sign-in. The site's top bar shows you your own Discord picture: your browser loads it from Discord's picture server (cdn.discordapp.com), so Discord sees that request; the site shows that picture to no one else.

The voting board. Leadership roles are voted on, except those the guild's leadership chooses without a public vote (the site marks them); an application for only such roles is not shown on the board. If you apply for a role that is voted on and tick the box that shows your application on the voting board, every member signed in to the site can read it there, under each role you chose that is voted on then or later: your Discord display name and, as its picture, the game's own class icon for the class you gave (the board shows the game's icons, never your Discord picture; the avatar reference Discord gave at sign-in stays stored with your account record, as said above), your class, role and where you play from, which roles you chose (first choice or backup), how many NA and EU raid evenings a week you can make, and your answers to the four questions about your experience, why Olympus, leading people, and the loot argument. Your main character, professions, logs, references, voice chat, hours, weekly schedule and the last free-text box are never shown there. An application leaves the board when you withdraw it or once the leadership has decided it. Votes on the board and people written in for a role are counted and seen only by the site's administrators; nobody else, including the applicants and the people written in, sees how anyone voted or any counts. Someone you write in is told the role the next time they sign in, never by whom.

Appointed roles. The site's administrators may fill a role by appointment instead of the board and type the holder's name (up to 40 characters, tied to no Discord account), and they type a name only after that person agreed. An appointed name is public: anyone, signed in or not, sees it on the Roles page and wherever the site names the role, the site's public settings carry it, and search engines and web archives on the open web may copy it; removing it here cannot recall their copies. An appointment gives no powers in the bot, the site or Discord, and an administrator clears it after the beta has closed. The dated log records which roles were appointed and how many names were saved, never the names (and whether the site-wide notice was set, never its text). Build .115 rewrites the entries written before it the same way, once, when it first starts; if that fails, the failure is logged and the rewrite is tried again at a later start, and after installing it the owner checks that the rewrite is recorded as done and that no such entry still holds a name or a notice's text.

Voting-board sharing and directory listing are separate choices. For names entered by staff, staff must obtain the person's agreement before publication; the server records the staff confirmation, not the person's agreement itself. Discord sign-in is permission to identify your Discord account and check this server membership. It does not verify your age or automatically authorize every separate publication of your information.

A full guild. While Olympus I is full (the officers' latest roster export counts the member limit, when that export is complete, passed the size checks and is less than two days old; or an invite was just refused for lack of space), /verify-status says so, with the hour of that export or refusal, to an account waiting on an invite, and the site's Home and Apply pages say so to an account waiting in the invite queue (with its place) or without a confirmed character (with that hour). Your own characters' places in the invite queue are shown to you, and only you. No other member sees your place. Officers see the invite queue itself (the character names, the accounts and their order) in their staff commands, and the bot's private staff channels record each confirmation (the character and the account, never a code) and, while the guild is full, how many are waiting.

The community pages

The site has community pages that the guild's staff switch on one at a time; a page that is off shows nothing and takes no new entries or actions, while what it already holds stays in your copy, keeps the lifetime stated below, is cleaned up on the same schedule, can still be erased on request, and stays in the dated log as before. Each stores only what you enter there or what the officers record, under your Discord user ID. Nothing on these pages changes your Discord roles, your guild membership or your access by itself: the officers decide those, and this software keeps the records.

Member directory and crafting offers. You may list, for other guild members, a main character, a raid role, up to four professions with a skill level, up to ten alts you claim, and up to fifty crafting offers. They are shown to members signed in to the site whose own character the officers' roster export confirms, only while you keep them listed and remain such a member yourself; other members see you by your display name and a random reference, never your Discord user ID. A character name you enter is a label until it matches the officers' record of your characters; a name bound to another account is refused. Officers may confirm or reject a claimed alt. A profile whose owner is no longer a confirmed member stops being shown at once and is deleted, with everything under it, by the scheduled cleanup from thirty days later.

The leadership directory. The site's administrators may list, for each Olympus guild (Olympus I to Olympus X), its Guild Master and officers by the names they type, and they list a name only after that person agreed. Confirmed members can read it. A listing is a record only: it gives no powers in the bot, the site or Discord. An administrator clears it after the beta has closed and changes it when the leadership changes. Anyone listed there or appointed to a role (see the guild site, above) can ask any Olympus officer, or use the privacy account help page, to have the name removed or corrected, and an administrator does it at once: the name is cleared, or replaced by “Name withheld”, which keeps an appointed role appointed.

News. When the site's administrators switch it on (it is off until they do), confirmed members can read a News page. It shows notices the administrators post for the whole guild, and figures the site works out by itself: whether Olympus I has room, with the hour of the roster export or refused invite that says so; how many characters joined and left the guild in the last day and the last seven days, compared between complete roster exports that passed the size checks; how many applications were first saved and how many decisions were saved in the same periods, where a count from one to four is shown only as “fewer than 5”; when the leadership directory last changed, never its names; the beta's dates; and the site's release notes. The figures are counts and times, never a name or anyone's place in line, and are worked out at most every three hours, only while the page is on. The page also lists the next events by their start times and the titles their organizers gave them; a title is free text, shown exactly as the calendar already shows it to confirmed members (see Calendar and attendance). A notice is different: it is free text an administrator writes for the whole guild, shown as plain text, and the site does not check what it says. The administrators name a member in a notice only with that member's agreement, and anyone a notice names can ask any Olympus officer, or use the privacy account help page, to have it changed or deleted, and an administrator does it at once. A notice is shown for the period the administrator chose, from 1 to 90 days after it was first posted (judged by the database's own clock in every read), and then deleted by the scheduled cleanup; an administrator may change or delete it sooner. Members see when a notice was posted and last changed, not who wrote it: the administrator who posted it and the one who last changed it are recorded for the staff only. A notice that was deleted or ran out leaves behind only its id, who posted it and when, until 120 days after it was posted, and a page can post a new notice only within 30 days of being opened, so a page opened earlier cannot post it again. The dated log records that a notice was posted, changed or deleted and how many are shown, never a title or a text.

Calendar and attendance. Organizers (the site's administrators and the members named as organizers) create events: a title, details, start, duration, capacity and the roles wanted. You answer yes, tentative or no with a character and a raid role, and you can give or change that answer only before the event starts, while the calendar is on and you are a confirmed member; your answer, display name and character are visible to confirmed members looking at the event, and it holds a place only while you remain one. After an event started an organizer may record attendance (present, possibly late or having left early; absent; excused; or unknown). Attendance never feeds roles, eligibility or any penalty. An event, its answers, attendance and history are deleted by the scheduled cleanup from thirty days after it ends (a cancellation brings that forward).

Trial reviews. Officers may record a trial about your account: when it began, when its review is due, whether it was extended, and how it concluded (passed, or ended with a fixed reason). You can read your own trial's status and dates on the site, never the sponsor or the officers involved. A trial is kept thirty days after it concluded, or after its review was due if it never did; from that moment no page shows it and no new copy contains it, and the row is deleted in the scheduled cleanup (bounded batches, so it may take more than one run).

Restriction cases, the watch-list and the return review. Officers may record a case about a Discord account — a ban, a removal for conduct, or a removal for unpaid guild dues — with its dates, review and appeal decisions, and may record that the same account signed in again after the case was set. A case may carry a watch-list: the names and in-game identifiers of that account's characters, copied from the officers' own record, so the officers can see when another account later holds one of them. Watch-list rows are kept for at most twelve months per member (one period, which an officer may renew with a fixed reason), never longer than their case. These are staff records and review evidence only: nothing here bans, removes or recognizes anyone by itself. A removal case ends at its expiry (at most two years), a ban case when an officer resolves it; a resolved case is kept thirty days. Past those moments no page shows the case and no new copy contains it.

Departure review items. When a character bound to your account leaves or is removed from the in-game guild, a review item (the character, whether it left or was removed, the date) exists for thirty days so an officer can acknowledge it or open a case from it. Nothing is decided by the item itself.

Guild dues (the contribution ledger). When the officers switch it on, the site keeps a ledger of the weekly guild dues in in-game copper: your weeks (eligible or exempt, due, paid, acknowledged), the payments the officers record (amount, date and source; the payer name as written in the game is for officers only), and your acknowledgements of a notice. The site shows you a mail reference to put in the subject of the in-game mail so an officer can match it to you; it is a matching aid, not proof of payment — the officer records the payment. Nothing is sent to you or taken from you by this software; an officer may record that you were removed in game for unpaid dues, which is a record, not an action. Ledger records are kept for the period the officers set when they switch it on (between one day and ten years, fixed when each record is written). Past its period a week is answered to no one; past its period a recorded payment's private details (its source identifier, the payer name as written, who recorded it) become unavailable at once and its unused remainder can never pay a later week, with one narrow exception kept on purpose: while a payment already paid a week that is itself still within its period, the minimum the accounting needs stays with that week: the payment's kind (an officer's entry, a mail, a bank log), the amount it paid that week and the part already retired, its date and status, and which week it paid; nothing more, so the week's paid state is never falsified, and it goes when the week goes.

Discord and Blizzard authenticate their own accounts; Olympus does not ask for those account passwords. Discord sign-in does not request your email address or your Discord messages. Free-text fields, including applications, privacy inbox conversations and staff notes, store the text supplied and may contain email addresses or other personal information. Include only what is needed; do not send passwords, tokens or identity documents. A connection IP address is used for short-lived rate limiting; it is not written into the privacy inbox case record. This describes the application code, not a promise about all network data processed by its hosting provider. Olympus does not process real-money payments. The in-game copper the guild dues ledger records is described under the community pages. In the game, the officers' addon reads the verification codes whispered or mailed to an officer's character, the game's own guild join and leave messages, the guild roster and the result of each invite it sends. A companion program on that officer's computer reads the game client's chat log file (which holds whatever chat that client logs) and the addon's saved data, and forwards to the bot only what it extracts from them: the code, the character and guild names, the invite outcomes, the roster export, whether that officer is in the world, and a timing measurement of the log file itself. By its code nothing else is sent, and the chat log stays on that officer's computer; this describes the software as written, not a guarantee about the game client.

If you are banned from verifying, the bot keeps your Discord user ID and the reason; the characters bound to that account stay bound to it.

Battle.net. Battle.net sign-in is currently switched off. No active Battle.net login data collection or Forever ownership check is provided. Any records left by an earlier link are legacy records, not proof of Forever character ownership; ask Olympus volunteer administration about those Olympus records. Discord separately keeps its linked-role connection under your Discord account. You can remove that connection in Discord's Settings under Connections. No future Battle.net login retention period is promised while the feature is disabled.

Renames Blizzard required. When a roster export shows that a character bound to you was renamed (the same character under a new name), the link follows the character and the site's administrators see the old and new name. If an administrator marks the rename as required by Blizzard, the bot records that decision (your Discord user ID, both names, that character's key and in-game identifier, when it was decided, by whom, and when it was closed): the character is unbound and has to be verified again, your site application is set back so that you submit it again, your Guild Member role is removed and held unless another of your characters is in the guild, and /verify-status tells you why; an administrator approves once your new application was accepted and the character verified again, or withdraws the decision. The record is deleted thirty days after it was approved or withdrawn; while it is open it stays.

Where it goes

Data lives in a Cloudflare D1 database owned by the guild's officers, in Cloudflare's infrastructure, together with the site's files and the Worker's operational logs. The reviewed error logger reduces error details to categories and bounded status codes. Some error paths also log truncated request paths or command and component labels, which can include user-supplied text. Cloudflare's operational logging and retention settings apply to that output. A working copy of the invite queue, the addon's saved data and the game's chat log sit on the computer of the officer who sends the invites. The bot talks to Discord as part of its ordinary work: it reads members' roles and nicknames, grants and removes the Guild Member role, maps verified current native guild ranks to Olympus rank roles and the Guild Leader, Officer or Raid Leader access roles where the configured rank profile permits, sets nicknames where that is switched on, bans where an officer orders it, writes a short reason into Discord's own audit log for those changes, refreshes the Discord usernames and display names of linked members, re-checks a signed-in member's server membership about hourly after a save, and posts the notices and staff messages described below; Discord keeps its own records of all that under its terms. While Battle.net sign-in is switched off, Blizzard receives nothing from this bot. Nothing is sold or used for advertising, training or analytics, and there are no third-party trackers on this page; beyond what this page describes, nothing is shared.

Who can see it

Guild officers, through the bot's own commands and their in-game addon, can see which Discord account a character belongs to, its Discord username and display name, and how its verification went. The same lookup tells them whether you applied on the guild site, for which role and how far it got, and the character names you reserved. The site's administrators can see everything you entered on the site. Other members can see that you have signed up there (when they search for someone to write in or list as a friend), if you put a leadership application on the voting board, what the board shows, and what you list for them on the community pages (your directory profile and crafting offers, your calendar answers), as those sections say; nothing else you entered. Confirmed members can also read the News page while it is on: the figures the site works out by itself, which are counts and times and never your name or your place in the invite queue; the next events with the titles their organizers gave them, as the calendar shows them; and the notices the administrators write for the whole guild, which name a member only with that member's agreement (see News, above, to have one changed or deleted). If you hold an appointed role, anyone, signed in or not, sees your name as its holder, as said under the guild site. Beyond that, everyone else in the server sees only what the bot shows in public: your server nickname, where the staff have switched that on, which it then sets to your character's name when you are admitted; and the short notices in the server's bot notices channel (#olympus-notices) that mention you when your access changes. The Terms of Service list which notices those are; anything you might not want read out only says there is an update, and /verify-status shows the detail to you alone. The bot's log channel is private to staff.

The site's administrators also use the staff pages of the community features: the directory's claims review, trials, cases and their watch-lists, departure items, the dues ledger with the payer names and sources of recorded payments, the privacy inbox cases, the News notices with who posted and last changed each, and two staff views that change nothing: a coverage report (the latest roster export next to what the bot and the site already know about each bound account) and the counts behind the officer digest. Every change a staff member makes is recorded in the dated log under their Discord user ID; reading is not. Once a day the bot may post to a staff channel how many things wait for an officer, as counts only, never a name or an ID. A staff rank-planning page keeps its draft in that administrator's own browser and stores nothing on the server; that browser copy outlives signing out and any deletion on the server until the administrator clears their browser's data.

Councillor browser verification

AddOn-free members send their website verification code as an ordinary in-game whisper to an online qualified High Councillor. The councillor runs the AddOn and browser scanner; the browser holds a non-extractable signing key locally and the service stores its public key with a fixed twenty-four-hour lease. A separately accepted automatic scanner session lasts at most five minutes and ten proofs. Signing needs that councillor's game and browser to be running; Cloudflare alone cannot observe game membership. Proofs are councillor attestations, not Blizzard-issued ownership certificates.

The service keeps the request, challenge, account and generation, character GUID and name, native rank, roster reference, proof digest and signed role-settlement outcome. Current server checks, the configured native-rank profile, fresh complete roster evidence and one-use proof authority govern bot effects. Lost effects are reconciled without blindly repeating them. Browser keys remain in the councillor's browser until revoked or its local storage is cleared; server-side account erasure cannot reach that local copy. Account downloads omit public-key material, authentication secrets, proof digests and counterpart identities.

Getting rid of it

Open Account data controls for your current site session, or connect Discord for privacy actions. The separate connection asks Discord only to identify your account. It does not require server membership or create a guild-site account, and grants no guild, staff or bot role access. It lasts twelve minutes, with a separate one-use form for a curated copy and for serving-account erasure. Its authentication handshake expires after five minutes. Short-lived authentication hashes and form credentials are not included in downloads.

Explicitly requesting erasure closes the current account generation to ordinary site saves and new bot-managed role grants. A bounded scheduled job first confirms that your bot-managed Olympus Guild Member role is absent or removes it through the central role writer. If Discord is unavailable, the role outcome is unknown, an old operation is unresolved, or an unclassified store or ambiguous reference is present, completion is held. Manually assigned staff permissions need human handling. Keep the request ID and private status code: they read only that request until its original deadline and never restore ordinary account access. A lost response does not prove rollback or completion.

Qualified serving-account erasure removes the account and application, linked characters and their identifiers, requests and queue entries, attributable roster rows and notes, first-seen rows, inactive relay references, votes and friends, reserved names, registered community records, verification keys and proofs, settled role records, attributable operational actions, and the short-lived privacy connection. It scrubs covered staff-actor pointers and structured references. Typed appointments, directory names and arbitrary text are not assumed to identify your account; ambiguous references can hold completion and need attended correction. An independent later game census can contain current game facts again, without restoring your erased account link. Unbinding alone and leaving Discord are not erasure.

Separate safety purposes. An active verification ban and its necessary reason, unresolved safety cases and their watch records can remain under their separate rules. For your own rejected guild application or membership, erasure retains a keyed, minimized account marker with the fixed reason and original decision date for up to 365 days from that decision. It does not retain your application, name or rejection explanation, does not mean a general ban, and retries do not extend its deadline. A separate restore-suppression record holds the retired Discord account identifier and generation for 366 days from erasure, to prevent an old recovery copy from reviving that generation.

Discord messages. The service tracks bounded bot-created review, notice, guild-log, event-publication and reminder pointers it can attribute. It requests cleanup and confirms absence; a lost send or deletion response is kept as unresolved custody without automatic resend. Known pointers can continue cleanup after local account erasure. Unknown outcomes or unresolved records beyond their original deadline need attended resolution and are not silently called erased. Other people's messages and copies remain with their holders. The status explicitly separates serving-account completion, Discord cleanup and recovery copies.

Automatic retention. Inactive site accounts with no active linked character and dated inactive bot accounts become eligible for serving erasure after twelve months. Unsuccessful applications, ended links, unused requests, released reserved names, departed-account references, inactive relays and expired operational history use bounded cleanup with their original clocks. Undated legacy evidence is held for review. Beta requests and first-seen dates age from the eventual beta cutoff only after the attended reset confirms that cutoff; the original dates are preserved. An event cannot be scheduled more than 366 days beyond its original creation to perpetuate its records. Existing shorter trial, case, dues and News lifetimes continue to apply.

Where a record has a stated read cutoff, the database clock controls availability. Physical deletion follows in bounded scheduled batches and can need several runs or wait for a schema or provider hold. Profiles become unavailable when their owner no longer qualifies and are cleaned thirty days later. Events, answers and attendance are cleaned thirty days after the event ends. Unresolved safety cases, provider outcomes and active bans have separate purposes. Cleanup does not recall a downloaded, browser, game-client, officer-computer or provider copy.

Existing private cases. New privacy-inbox requests are replaced by the account controls above. Existing conversations can still be read with their case ID and private code at Read an existing case. The code controls that conversation only, not an account. Cases keep their fixed-at-creation inactivity policy; messages or staff changes can extend an open case, reads do not, and closing fixes its deadline. Scheduled cleanup removes expired cases. Ask an Olympus officer for attended handling of unattributable names, ambiguous records, unresolved external custody or staff permissions.

Recovery copies. Serving erasure does not physically rewrite a private database export or Cloudflare's observed up-to-30-day recovery history. Only the newest privately restored and verified export is kept; a superseded export is destroyed after its exact approved cleanup, and each export has a 365-day maximum age. Acceptance of its applicable release can dispose of it sooner. Until disposal or expiry, a copy can contain the data as captured. These are separate operator obligations, not an automatic all-copy erasure claim.

Recovery is attended: keep the site closed before restoring and until subsequent erasures and the generation suppression records have been replayed. Reapply the News suppression and changes, and restore the current typed appointments and leadership-directory settings so a removed or corrected name stays corrected. Check the settings-audit name rewrite before reopening. Never treat an older database as current membership, role, proof or privacy authority. The owner verifies each recovery copy's age and records replacement and cleanup receipts; unresolved custody remains explicitly open.

Keep any saved continuation private and use the same original site session before it expires.
