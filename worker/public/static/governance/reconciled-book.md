# 1 Guild Charter of Olympus

**ADOPTED GUILD CHARTER · R7 · 13 OCTOBER 2026**

Version: R7  Adoption date: 13 October 2026  Effective date: 13 October 2026

This Charter establishes a voluntary World of Warcraft: Forever community. It is a community governance document, not a contract of employment, a government instrument, or a grant of real-world legal authority. Its rules are adopted by the authorized guild administration. Individual appointment and access decisions remain separately recorded; adopting this text does not assign a holder, change a game rank or grant system access.

## 1 1 Purpose and membership

Olympus exists to make shared play dependable, welcoming, and enjoyable across its affiliated guilds. Its purposes are cooperation in PvP and raiding, sound stewardship of shared resources, support for crafting and learning, and community events. Membership does not require streaming, donating, raiding, or surrendering an account to another person. Social players, crafters, levelers, and competitive players are members of the same community.

Admission follows the published application and verification process. Initiates receive a named mentor and a clear review date. Advancement reflects the relevant published criteria; friendship, payment, and an officer's private preference are not substitutes. An alt remains linked to its verified owner and does not create a second vote or duplicate entitlement where a rule counts people rather than characters.

Members must observe Blizzard's applicable rules and the community's published conduct rules. For a staff-confirmed Blizzard-mandated character rename, that character must submit a new application, receive a new review, and complete fresh in-game verification. An ordinary rename that preserves the established GUID link does not itself require reapplication. A hold on one character is not an invented finding against every other character of its owner.

## 1 2 Document authority

Applicable platform terms and rules govern all community activity. Beneath them, the Royal Charter and Guild Charter form one constitutional settlement; the remaining order is Guild Statute, bylaws, duly adopted ordinances, then individual appointment instruments and event rules. A lower document may implement a higher one but cannot contradict it. The joint Charters prevail over inconsistent legacy charts, informal instructions, or announcements.

The Royal Charter provides ceremonial expression of that settlement and creates no additional royal or legal powers. The two Charters must be adopted and interpreted consistently; a substantive conflict requires published constitutional correction, not a secret preference for one text. Letters Patent establish only the appointment or affiliation stated in their operative fields. Daily news cannot amend rules or quietly appoint a person.

Every operative instrument must identify its version, approving authority, adoption date, effective date, and any superseded text. Material changes require publication and a reasonable opportunity for comment. Constitutional recommendations require two thirds of the total non-vacant High Council seats, followed by Founder ratification. Urgent temporary orders must state their necessity, scope, expiry, and reviewing authority. No retrospective rule may turn previously permitted conduct into a disciplinary offence.

## 1 3 Founder and final authority

Asmon is the charted Founder and final Olympus-wide authority, subject to this Charter's publication, review, and recusal requirements. The Founder ratifies the common constitution, appoints the High Council, confirms guild affiliation and Guild Masters, and resolves reserved constitutional questions. Final authority carries responsibility to give reasons for consequential decisions and to preserve the distinction between community leadership and private access to records.

The Founder may return a measure for reconsideration or issue a reasoned, recorded decision within the Charters. A case in which the Founder is complainant, material witness, or personally interested receives review by uninvolved adjudicators; the Founder does not act as fact-finder. Any later constitutional intervention must identify its grounds and the independent recommendation. Final authority cannot override platform rules or authorize retaliation against a person using the review process.

## 1 4 High Council and executive coordination

The High Council is selected through accepted appointments rather than earned automatically through activity. It advises the Founder, coordinates common policy, proposes the budget, reviews departmental performance and hears matters reserved by the Statute. High Council is also the second native character rank in the adopted ten-rank ladder. A Council seat, its mandate and eligible vote, a character's native rank and actual system permissions are separate records. A rank label creates no Council seat; this book and an unissued warrant assign no rank or access. The Treasury office remains an accepted High Council appointment, within the Leader / High Council tier, not a separate native rank. A native rank does not create that office or its vote. The documented High Council bank preset includes Withdraw Gold and Modify Bank Tabs for all characters holding that rank; actual application must be separately authorized and verified, with the auditor conflict in section 1.7 resolved.

Council quorum requires eligible participants present to exceed half of the total non-vacant seats, with at least three eligible participants. Recused seats remain in the denominator; recusal never lowers the three-person floor. Ordinary decisions use a majority of eligible votes cast at a quorate meeting. Abstentions are recorded but are not affirmative or negative votes; at least three eligible participants must remain present. A tied motion fails. If fewer than three are eligible, obtain an independent panel recommendation under the Statute; the panel gains no Council votes and cannot bypass quorum. An unconflicted appointing authority selects it. No person may determine the merits of their own case. Vacancies and recusals must be recorded.

Co-Guild Master, Vice Guild Master and Chief of Staff are scoped appointments, not additional native ranks. A warrant must identify whether any duties are explicitly combined or held separately, the delegated and reserved decisions, reporting line, limits and absence arrangements. They may coordinate daily work, agendas, local Guild Master liaison and continuity only within that written remit. Neither a title nor candidate consent replaces the Founder, confers permanent reserved powers or grants command over every guild. Each portfolio may have a named deputy. A deputy acts only within a written standing delegation or a recorded temporary substitution. Neither deputyship nor seniority creates automatic succession to the permanent office.

## 1 6 Justice and review

The Council of Justice handles conduct, disputes, appeals, and compliance independently of the department structure. High Councillors without another portfolio form its default membership, but case access and adjudicative authority require designation. A portfolio holder is not entitled to decide a complaint concerning their own department.

A member facing a consequential sanction receives notice, an intelligible account of the relevant evidence, an opportunity to respond, a reasoned outcome, and an appeal route. Private evidence may be summarized to protect another person; the panel must still assess it. Conflicted members recuse. Appeals go to people uninvolved in the original decision. A necessary emergency measure must be limited and reversible, expire after 24 hours unless an unconflicted authorized reviewer approves a written renewal before expiry, and receive independent review within 48 hours. Every renewal states necessity, scope and an expiry no more than 24 hours later; the initiating officer cannot self-renew. Good-faith criticism, complaints and appeals are not emergency grounds by themselves. Restrictions and bans are deliberate authorized decisions, never automatic punishment inferred from an unpaid ledger or a technical flag.

# 2 Royal Charter of Olympus

**CEREMONIAL CHARTER · ADOPTED WITH THE GUILD RULES · R7**

This instrument uses the language of a royal charter as guild fiction and ceremony. Olympus is not a sovereign realm. It creates no real-world nobility, royal status, public office, territorial claim, immunity, or legal privilege. Its operative meaning is confined to the ratified Guild Charter and community rules.

## 2 1 Preamble and foundation

Let it be known among the guilds and companions of Olympus that many guilds shall share one fellowship, and that no banner shall diminish the worth of those who bear another within it. By the assent recorded below, we establish Olympus as a common house of play, service, learning, and mutual regard.

We recognize Asmon as Founder and final authority within the published settlement. We establish the High Council to advise and coordinate, the four core portfolios to serve the guilds, and the Council of Justice to consider disputes with independence and restraint. Each Guild Master shall govern their own guild under the common Charter; each minister shall coordinate through willing and duly appointed emissaries. Service shall be measured by fulfilled duties, not by the grandeur of a title.

## 2 2 Liberties and obligations

Every member shall have the benefit of published rules, consistent application, a hearing before consequential judgment, and a route of appeal. No treasury contribution shall purchase office or immunity. No office shall excuse harassment, deceit, favoritism, or misuse of another person's records. Casual fellowship, craft, mentoring, and competitive achievement shall each have an honored place.

Those entrusted with resources shall keep faithful records. Those entrusted with command shall state its limits. Those entrusted with judgment shall disclose conflicts and recuse. Those entrusted with publicity shall distinguish fact, proposal, and ceremony. A ceremonial seal may authenticate a community record; it shall not substitute for an approving signature, a version, or a lawful platform permission.

## 2 3 Service  succession  and preservation

We permit written appointments for ministers, deputies, emissaries, tactical leaders, mentors, and other necessary servants of the fellowship. Their powers extend only as far as their warrants. On resignation, expiry, removal, or a declared conflict, they shall surrender the delegated duty and assist an orderly handover without destroying records or retaining access by custom.

Let this Royal Charter be read with the Guild Charter as one constitutional settlement, subordinate to applicable platform rules. Ceremonial words confer no wider powers than their shared operative provisions. A substantive inconsistency requires published correction under the constitutional adoption process. This ceremonial instrument is adopted as part of R7; actual personal appointments and permissions still require their separate instruments.

# Operational Charter — The Work of Olympus

## O1 The charge of service

Olympus is a fellowship of adventurers and a federation of guild households. Its strength rests on dependable people, clear promises and a welcome that survives a difficult raid night. The language of councils, ministers, warrants and emissaries gives our shared work a common identity. Every ceremonial title has a practical meaning: a person accepts a duty, receives a bounded mandate, keeps an intelligible record and answers for the result. No title purchases obedience outside that mandate.

The administration serves members who raid, fight in battlegrounds, craft, explore, level, roleplay or simply enjoy the company. A member's chosen activity determines the commitments they accept. Social members do not owe raid attendance. A crafter does not owe unpaid labour. A donation does not buy rank, an appointment, a favourable judgment or priority over published rules.

The adopted rules govern decisions from 13 October 2026. They do not retroactively create an offence, invent an old appointment or certify a feature as deployed. Actual game permissions, Discord roles, website access and financial custody each need the authorized operator and their own recorded implementation. Where the platform cannot implement a rule, the responsible officer records the limitation and uses a compatible procedure rather than claiming an imaginary control.

## O2 Four kinds of authority

Constitutional authority defines the common settlement and reserved decisions. A Council appointment defines a seat, mandate, eligible vote and term. A native rank classifies a verified character in the game. An operational permission allows a particular action in a particular system. The Founder and Patron holds the reserved constitutional responsibilities described in the Charters. The High Council coordinates common policy, resources and portfolio performance. Each affiliated Guild Master commands their own guild within the common obligations. A minister works through that Guild Master and appointed emissaries; a ministry does not acquire ownership of a guild, its bank or its members.

Every instruction should make its source plain. An event order names the event leader and duration. A financial approval names the purpose and ceiling. A case direction identifies the authorized panel and scope. A technical release names the approved artifact and operator. An ambiguous instruction is clarified before an irreversible action. A request to exceed a mandate returns to the competent authority; urgency does not silently enlarge it.

# Bylaws of Olympus

**Adopted operating procedures subordinate to the constitutional instruments and Guild Statute.** The periods, quorum rules, financial threshold and appointment terms govern administration; they do not claim that a software control exists or that game permissions have been applied.

## Bylaw B1   Appointments and the register

An appointment warrant records the office, guild or portfolio, duties, supervisor, boundaries, start date, review date, accepted appointee and required permissions. Ordinary appointments have a renewable six-month term unless the warrant specifies a shorter pilot. Technical custody may be reviewed more frequently. A vacancy remains vacant until an accepted appointment is recorded; chart labels are not warrants.

The Chief of Staff is accountable for the registers; the delegated Secretary or Registrar maintains the current office register and confidential permission register. An explicitly named acting registrar covers a vacancy without acquiring further authority. A recorded appointment, separately authorized permission change and verified readback are three distinct steps. Removal or expiry requires checking all actual access, service ownership and shared credentials without deleting needed records or disabling recovery.

## Bylaw B2   Council notice and quorum

Ordinary High Council meetings receive seven calendar days' notice with an agenda; urgent meetings receive at least twenty-four hours where practicable. The Secretary records current non-vacant voting seats before the meeting. Vacancies have no vote. Attendance, recusals and declared interests are recorded.

Quorum requires more than half of all current non-vacant voting seats and at least three eligible, unconflicted members. Recusal does not reduce the seat denominator or the protective floor. A councillor with several offices has one vote. Without quorum, members may discuss and recommend but cannot record a binding Council decision. If fewer than three unconflicted members are available, an unconflicted appointing authority commissions an independent panel recommendation. The panel acquires no Council votes and cannot bypass quorum. A conflicted Founder cannot decide the merits of their own case or overwrite the independent finding as its fact-finder.

## Bylaw B3   Motions  votes and constitutional recommendations

Ordinary motions pass by a majority of eligible votes cast at a quorate meeting. Abstentions are recorded but not counted as affirmative or negative votes; there must still be at least three eligible participants. A tie defeats the motion; the chair has no extra casting vote. Written votes use the same membership, notice and quorum rules and a stated closing time.

A proposed constitutional amendment, guild affiliation or permanent creation of a department requires affirmative votes from at least two-thirds of all current non-vacant Council seats, rounded upward, followed by the reserved approval required by the Charters. Publish adopted wording, effective date and reasons. Membership consultation informs these decisions but is not falsely reported as a referendum or unanimous consent.

## Bylaw B4   Minutes and reserved decisions

Minutes identify the proposal, authority, participants, conflicts, vote, reasons, implementation owner and review date. Circulate a draft within seven days and correct errors visibly. Publish the non-sensitive decision summary; protect case and financial evidence. A reserved decision by Asmon must identify whether it affirms, varies or returns a Council recommendation and give reasons. It is entered into the same register rather than conveyed as an unverifiable private instruction.

## Bylaw B5   Emergency measures

A specifically authorized officer may impose the narrowest reversible measure needed to address an immediate credible risk. Record the reason, affected capability and time as soon as practicable, notify the subject safely and seek an unconflicted review. The temporary measure expires after twenty-four hours unless an unconflicted authorized reviewer approves a written renewal before expiry. Each renewal records reasons, precise scope and an expiry no more than twenty-four hours later; the initiating officer cannot self-renew. Good-faith criticism, complaints and appeals alone are never emergency grounds. A reviewer independent of the initiating officer examines necessity within forty-eight hours.

If immediate removal is technically unavoidable, preserve the evidence and an available appeal route. Emergency authority cannot permanently amend the constitution, appoint a council, dispose of substantial resources or erase a complaint. Extensions require an explicit next review date; a rolling hold is not a substitute for a hearing.

## Bylaw B6   Intake  trials and mentoring

Recruitment staff assign a contact and explain which steps are pending. The introductory review occurs within fourteen days of admission. A single further fourteen-day extension requires recorded reasons and support goals; thereafter decide membership or explain why the process cannot be completed. An existing member's raid trial assesses that activity without cancelling unrelated membership.

Mentors help with rules, verification, class resources and first events. Trial criteria concern demonstrated conduct and the chosen role, not undisclosed attendance quotas. Staff record minimal outcomes and the next review date; neither silence nor a missing export counts as proof of failure.

## Bylaw B7   Resource authorization and audit

A substantial transaction is an unbudgeted transfer exceeding ten gold, or any transfer exceeding ten percent of the last reconciled unrestricted balance, whichever threshold is reached first. Apply this procedural threshold to combined related transfers over seven days to prevent splitting. Where no reliable balance exists, treat every non-routine transfer as substantial until reconciliation.

Substantial transactions require two recorded approvals: the responsible Treasurer or guild master and an independent, unconflicted authorized approver. Neither recipient may approve their own payment. Record authorization before the separately permitted custodian executes it. Publish routine budget ceilings; perform weekly reconciliation and monthly independent review. Suspected loss preserves logs and triggers investigation, not a public accusation. The auditor remains independent of withdrawal custody.

## Bylaw B8   Complaints and case administration

A complainant may approach a designated local officer, welfare contact or Justice case officer. Acknowledge a received complaint within three working days, identify an unconflicted case owner and explain likely timing. Attempt mediation only when suitable and voluntary; serious safety concerns need not pass through mediation first. Limit access to those handling the case.

Case records state allegation, applicable rule, evidence sources, response, finding, consequence, implementation authority and appeal deadline. Separate administrative membership verification from a misconduct finding. A moderator enforces only the recorded measure within actual permissions; broader enforcement requires a new decision or justified emergency measure.

## Bylaw B9   Operational cadence and continuity

Each active guild reviews intake and urgent member needs weekly. Portfolio heads and emissaries coordinate fortnightly; the Council reviews budgets, vacancies, risks and service performance monthly. These are administrative service cadences, not compulsory play hours. An unavailable officeholder designates a permitted deputy or asks for cover; essential custody has a documented recovery contact.

Technical releases need an identified owner, reviewed changes, recovery arrangements and current evidence of actual deployment. Daily news may report confirmed changes, forthcoming events and approved appointment notices. It shall label proposals and corrections, exclude confidential allegations and avoid claiming that a drafted rule is live.

## Bylaw B10   Adoption and implementation

R7 adoption is recorded on 13 October 2026 from the authorized guild administration’s explicit instruction. The record is an administrative adoption, not an invented Founder signature, unanimous Council vote or personal assent by every Guild Master. Future ratification or amendment follows the constituted Council and reserved approval rules. Record exact wording, authority, effective date, material objections and the actual decision; obtain any separately required affiliation or appointment acceptance from its real parties.

Then issue appointment warrants, review permissions individually, verify the implemented access and pilot new duties in one guild for six to eight weeks before wider adoption. No appointment is filled merely because this book describes it. Ratification authorizes policy; operational changes still require competent operators and the relevant approvals. Review the adopted package after the pilot and annually thereafter without rewriting the historical decision record.

## Article I.4 — Emergency powers and the watch of the Crown

### Section I.4.1 — Necessity and the narrowest measure

(a) An authorized officer may invoke an emergency measure only for an immediate credible risk to people, entrusted assets or operation of the guild's services. The record describes the concrete risk, supporting observations, affected capability, start time, initiating officer and why ordinary notice would not suffice. Good-faith criticism, a complaint, an appeal, declined attendance or unpaid voluntary recognition is never an emergency ground. A troubling rumour alone is not a finding of guilt.

(b) Choose a reversible restriction limited to the risk. A local channel hold does not imply a cross-branch ban; a suspected key problem does not authorize a roster purge. Preserve the original evidence and tell the affected person the safe summary and review route as soon as practicable. Safety may require redaction, but a secret accusation cannot become a permanent sanction without the fair process in Title II.

### Section I.4.2 — Expiry and independent review

(a) The existing twenty-four-hour expiry governs every temporary emergency measure. Before expiry, an unconflicted authorized reviewer may grant a written renewal stating continuing necessity, precise scope and a new expiry no more than twenty-four hours later. The initiating officer cannot self-renew. Independent review begins within the existing forty-eight-hour limit, even if a short measure has already ended; expiry does not excuse examination of an error.

(b) The Crown may call the council bell and seek urgent advice, but cannot use an emergency to appoint a Council, amend the constitution permanently, approve substantial private spending or destroy a complaint. A continuing dispute goes to ordinary proceedings with their notice and evidence protections. Every extension has a next review point; a chain of automatic renewals or an unexplained indefinite hold is forbidden. Technical inability to undo an effect is reported honestly and escalated for attended correction.

## Article I.5 — The royal register, succession and accountability

### Section I.5.1 — The clerk's evidentiary duty

(a) The register links each edict to its proposal, competent approval, exact edition, publication, implementation and later review. It distinguishes a contemporary original from a transcript, a member's report and an inference. Corrections append an explanation and preserve provenance. Public entries contain enough reason to understand the decision without exposing unnecessary account identifiers, private financial evidence or protected case details.

(b) The clerk circulates ordinary minutes within the established seven-day period and records a correction visibly when a participant disputes accuracy. Missing records remain missing; an attractive seal or later recollection is not substituted for an absent signature. Disagreement over the register goes to an unconflicted reviewer. Its service to the Crown is fidelity to the record, not embellishment of a preferred story.

### Section I.5.2 — Vacancy, recusal and the end of a mandate

(a) Resignation, expiry, lawful revocation or a declared conflict ends the affected delegation according to its instrument. An acting warrant identifies the temporary holder and end condition; no title alone appoints a successor. The outgoing custodian hands over open work, approved records and unresolved effects. Actual access removal is a separate operator task with a confirmed or uncertain outcome, never an assumption inferred from a changed chart.

(b) A Sovereign personally interested in a case does not determine its facts. The independent recommendation, any reserved constitutional response and its reasons remain separate entries. Members retain the right to seek correction and appeal without retaliation. The golden banner is a promise of accountable service, not immunity from the laws beneath which it flies.

## Article II.2 — Deliberation, quorum and reasons

### Section II.2.1 — Notice and the council table

(a) Ordinary meetings give seven calendar days' notice and an intelligible agenda; urgent meetings give at least twenty-four hours where practicable and record why shorter notice was necessary. The clerk establishes the current total of non-vacant voting seats and the eligible participants. A matter cannot be slipped into a different heading to deny a person reasonable notice. Sensitive annexes may be limited to competent reviewers while a non-sensitive description remains available.

(b) Quorum is more than half of the total non-vacant seats, with at least three eligible, unconflicted participants. Recusal does not lower either the denominator or that floor. Where fewer than three can act, an unconflicted appointing authority seeks an independent panel recommendation; the panel acquires no Council votes and cannot circumvent quorum. Discussion without quorum is advice, expressly recorded as non-binding.

### Section II.2.2 — Decision and publication

(a) Ordinary motions require a majority of eligible votes cast at a quorate meeting. Abstentions are recorded without becoming affirmative votes; a tie fails and the chair has no second casting vote. A written resolution uses the same eligibility, notice and quorum requirements and states a closing time. Constitutional recommendations retain the two-thirds-of-total-non-vacant-seats rule followed by Founder ratification. A simple majority cannot disguise a constitutional change as an ordinary act.

(b) Minutes name the proposal, authority, conflicts, attendance, vote, reasons, implementation owner and review date, and are circulated within seven days. A public summary distinguishes a decision from a recommendation, an executed action from a pending one and verified facts from unresolved claims. Case evidence and personal payment details remain private. Members may request correction of a material recording error without being accused of disloyalty.

## Article II.3 — Officer conduct and the independence of Justice

### Section II.3.1 — Duties of an officer

(a) An officer shall use entrusted powers for the published purpose, disclose a personal or financial interest, keep private material within authorized channels and refrain from threats, favouritism, impersonation and retaliation. A rare recipe, large donation, audience or friendship with leadership grants no exemption. The officer must identify when speaking for an office and when expressing a personal preference; ceremonial rank cannot turn that preference into law.

(b) A conflict includes being complainant, material witness, recipient, custodian under review or a close participant in the disputed event. Disclose it before receiving the merits bundle and withdraw from investigation, decision and appeal as appropriate. An unconflicted authority records the replacement. Necessary factual assistance may be requested without restoring a vote. The person whose conduct is examined cannot select a favourable fact-finder or approve their own evidence exclusion.

### Section II.3.2 — Evidence stewardship and standard of proof

(a) The case owner records the source, date, context and integrity limits of each exhibit. Original messages or logs are preferred to cropped images; testimony is identified as testimony, and a technical observation is bounded by what was actually visible. A missing record, offline player, stale roster or unsuccessful network request proves neither misconduct nor absence of an entitlement. No private token, recovery key or irrelevant account information is requested as an exhibit.

(b) Ordinary contested facts are decided on the balance of probabilities: the panel explains why the supported account is more likely than not. A lasting exclusion or finding of serious dishonesty additionally requires clear, cogent and corroborated evidence proportionate to its gravity; an uncorroborated rumour cannot meet that protection. Emergency necessity is a separate provisional risk assessment, not a lower route to a final conviction. The panel records uncertainty and the evidence it could not obtain.

## Article II.4 — Complaints, hearings and the trial record

### Section II.4.1 — Intake and a meaningful answer

(a) A designated officer acknowledges a received complaint within three working days, identifies an unconflicted case owner and states the next expected step. Mediation is voluntary and appropriate only where safe; serious safety allegations need not pass through it. Intake records the alleged rule, approximate event, requested remedy and available sources, avoiding a second public allegation thread. The complainant is not promised a result before the evidence is reviewed.

(b) Before a consequential judgment, the subject receives the alleged breach, useful evidence summary, possible consequence and at least seven calendar days to respond, unless a narrow emergency measure is independently justified. Private sources may be protected by an intelligible summary and questions through the case owner. A decision cannot rest solely on undisclosed material. Reasonable extensions for availability or accessibility are recorded with a new date, without requiring a person to disclose unnecessary medical details.

### Section II.4.2 — The sitting of the Court

(a) A hearing may be written or a private meeting with an agreed record. The chair states the issues, confirms recusals, receives each side's relevant account and permits questions through a respectful procedure. No compelled public spectacle or trial by stream is permitted. Witnesses distinguish what they saw from what they were told. Recording or publication requires the applicable consent and policy; declining publicity is not evidence against a party.

(b) The written outcome addresses each material allegation, the rule in force at the time, evidence accepted or rejected with reasons, findings, proportionate remedy, actual enforcing authority and appeal deadline. The panel gives an expected decision date and explains any delay before it elapses; absent a more protective case arrangement, it aims to decide within fourteen calendar days after the response period closes. Delay never converts an unproved allegation into guilt or silently extends an emergency hold.

## Article II.5 — Penal measures and the Sentry Hold code

### Section II.5.1 — Proportionality and a ladder of remedies

(a) The `Moderation.lua` division implements only a separately authorized measure within actual platform capability. A corrective explanation, agreed restitution or warning should be considered before an activity restriction where the risk and gravity permit. A sanction identifies the conduct addressed, its scope and duration, review condition and person authorized to end it. A raid selection decision about current readiness is not silently repurposed as a community-wide disciplinary ban.

(b) The Court considers seriousness, demonstrated harm, intent where evidenced, recurrence and reasonable mitigation. It does not punish poverty, a waived obligation, refusal of optional tithe recognition, lawful criticism or a good-faith appeal. Native rank, directory standing, moderation safety roles and account privacy rights are separate capabilities. A decision affecting one does not authorize a general account purge or an invented rule against all linked characters.

### Section II.5.2 — Sentry Hold as protection, never a sentence in disguise

(a) Penal / Sentry Hold (Timeout) is a bounded protective or disciplinary label only when a competent decision gives it actual scope. A provisional Sentry Hold follows Title I's twenty-four-hour expiry, independent renewal and forty-eight-hour review. A final time-limited restriction requires the hearing record and stated end date. The label alone applies no sanction and creates no new native rank. An absent or inaccessible implementation control remains unavailable, not presumed effective.

(b) The operator records the original request and actual acknowledgement. If an external answer is lost, preserve the original operation and check its permitted status; do not blindly send a replacement restriction or removal. Unknown stays unknown. A technical failure cannot lengthen the sentence by stealth. Corrective access changes require their competent operator, and successful removal of a website label does not prove a game or Discord permission has been restored.

## Article II.6 — Appeals, correction and restoration

### Section II.6.1 — A new ear for the petition

(a) The subject may appeal within fourteen calendar days of receiving the written decision for unfair procedure, material new evidence, an unreasonable finding or a disproportionate consequence. An uninvolved reviewer acknowledges within seven days and supplies an expected decision date. The original investigator, decision-maker and any materially interested person do not hear the appeal. If an impartial body is unavailable, the competent unconflicted authority appoints one rather than recycling the same panel.

(b) The appeal may uphold, vary or return the matter for reconsideration and gives reasons on the grounds raised. No automatic increase in sanction follows an appeal. The reviewer states what relief is effective immediately and what depends on an attended platform correction.

### Section II.6.2 — Clearing the record and learning from error

(a) A corrected finding is appended to the original case and reflected in any member-readable summary without needless republication of the allegation. (b) Justice reviews recurring procedural failures and recommends a prospective rule or training change. Case retention follows its actual purpose and published privacy policy; a court title is not a blanket audit-retention exemption. Authenticated serving-account erasure and narrow safety, unresolved-effect or recovery custody retain their own rules. The Court safeguards both the right to be heard and the right not to have private evidence turned into a permanent public memorial.
